# Direction contract

Seed reference: `seed:8578b41bb97826f8`

## World

The Finding Aid: special-collections finding aids, archival index dockets,
one-pixel rules, finding-aid tabs, and a single oxidised-red evidence key.
The product remains an Operate surface. The world contributes type, palette,
density, and one signature move; navigation and controls remain standard web
patterns.

## First viewport

A three-column research desk. The dark archival navigation exposes the complete
product through numbered folio entries. The left rail is the collection index.
The center is a cited research conversation with a standard composer. The right
rail is the evidence register with numbered page-level entries. The header
shows the selected paper, retrieval route, current model, and library size.

## Signature interaction

Evidence is treated as a ruled finding-aid register: each citation receives a
numbered key on a vertical oxidised-red line, and the same numbers are embedded
in the answer. State is carried through line weight and filled marks as well as
color. Answers can be copied without losing their inline citation markers.

## Cross-surface reach

- Workspace: collection index, cited conversation, evidence register.
- Document library: ruled accession table and indexing queue.
- Citation Graph: graph canvas, metric ledger, and inspector.
- Evidence Audit: audit docket, evidence register, claim rulings.
- Administration: user and invite registers.
- System Status: service ledger.
- Authentication: archival cover and ruled access form.

## Responsive composition

The side rail becomes a horizontal folio index. Work areas become ordered
vertical sections instead of shrinking three columns. The composer remains
fluid, the evidence rail stacks, and no action depends on hover.

## Risk

Archive references can become decorative clutter if the operational hierarchy
is not strict. The design keeps standard controls, direct labels, and clear
state semantics so the world never outranks the task.
